# Intelligence briefing — case case-20261025-084715-fa6fe3

## Executive summary

Established fact: the Orvan Corridor Agreement expired on 10 September 2026 without renewal or replacement (ORV-0016, tier 1). An earlier renewal report was withdrawn (ORV-0013). Inference from trade data: flows on the Orvan route to Midgard and Tolan fell sharply and stayed low through December 2026. Midgard wheat on the Orvan route was 104.2 to 216.2 USD M below the same months of 2025 (Nov-Dec to Sep-Dec; CALC-54746bbc2bce35cc, CALC-2471c1be02c2601c; tier-1 data). Midgard maize fell with a lag, explained by delayed delivery terms, and was 105.7 to 126.4 USD M lower (Nov-Dec to Oct-Dec; CALC-c4d8aa36b4090272, CALC-8b68f9523d386676). Tolan wheat on the Orvan route fell by at least 46.5 USD M in Nov-Dec (CALC-9b914fa4d6443634; tier-2 data). Its Sep-Dec high bound is not yet verified, so the full range is marked insufficient. Tolan maize exposure is unknown, not zero, because no Tolan maize trade data exist. All figures are gross declines in trade value on the Orvan route, not economic loss. They mix the corridor expiry with a separate dock strike from 14 September (ORV-0032), and they do not net substitute supply. Vanir wheat reached Midgard in larger volumes from November (ORV-0027/P14), and millers report rail deliveries via Ravik (ORV-0022/P3). Millers call the substitute supply reliable enough for current demand but more expensive to handle (ORV-0022/P5). Net exposure is therefore lower than the gross figures by an amount that cannot yet be measured. Overall, Midgard is highly exposed on the Orvan route for both grains. Tolan wheat is clearly exposed but less precisely measured. Tolan maize is an evidence gap.

## Next-quarter outlook

For the next quarter, this outlook is conditional and rests on the stated assumptions, not on evidence after 25 January 2027. If the agreement remains unrenewed and the port bottleneck persists, Orvan route flows to Midgard and Tolan are likely to stay well below prior-year levels, as they did every month from September to December 2026. Buyers are contracting month by month (ORV-0017/P5), which leaves them exposed to continued disruption. Vanir wheat substitution was still rising at the end of the data, on short-term contracts, so net wheat exposure may narrow. However, rail capacity is reportedly limited and substitute supply is more expensive to handle. No maize substitution is measured, so Midgard maize exposure is the less cushioned of the two Midgard grains. Two developments would materially change this outlook: an end to the dock strike, or publication of the ministry's loadings-based port return. A change in Orvan route volumes after the strike ends would show how much of the decline was strike-driven rather than corridor-driven.

Assumptions:
- No replacement corridor arrangement is signed during the quarter; none was signed as of the as-of date (ORV-0016/P2).
- The dock strike and berth constraints persist at an unknown level; their end date is not in evidence.
- Vanir wheat substitution continues on short-term contracts at least at the level reached in November-December 2026.
- Ravik rail capacity remains limited, as reported, and is not quantified.
- No January 2027 or later trade data are used; the outlook extrapolates direction only, not magnitude.
